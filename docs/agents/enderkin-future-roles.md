# Future Enderkin roles

The Saboteur and Tank are approved creative directions. The behaviors and prototype limits below need validation in Tekkit Dev and owner approval before becoming production rules. Alpha.4 ships neither role. Allocate stable entity names, IDs, spawn eggs and save fields in their individual implementation changes.

## Saboteur prototype

A short Enderkin with quick, intermittent movement draws the player's attention away from the main assault. Test a footprint roughly 0.45 blocks wide and 1.1 tall, with a low silhouette, a bright hand pose and a distinct target-facing windup. Its direct attack is a low-damage escape tool. Its priority is a visible, accessible point of interruption. An exposed player can decide whether to abandon cover, send fire toward the Saboteur, or let the interruption expire.

Prototype with a disposable, opt-in ZTO transfer fixture. Its sender stops offering **new** transfers while a short in-memory lease applies. Receivers, pipes, inventories and in-flight payloads continue their normal simulation. Represent the effect visibly at the fixture during the lease. Use the same entity and visual sequence for a harmless decoy when there is no supported target. Measure whether players recognize the approach, windup and recovery before attaching the behavior to an installed network.

| Step | Proposed prototype contract |
| --- | --- |
| Acquire | Search only a loaded, bounded 8-block radius around the actor, on a staggered cadence of at most one scan per second. Consider only the fixture allowlist. Reject sealed destinations and unsupported or replaced targets. |
| Approach | Follow normal paths. A short blink of at most four blocks may reposition within the same accessible approach. Validate source-to-destination clearance and the destination footprint, floor and loaded chunks. Reject travel across closed doors, opaque walls or inaccessible height changes. Use a small fixed candidate list and at least two seconds between attempts. |
| Signal and interrupt | Expose the actor through a 25-tick windup with a visible link to the target. A landed player hit, lost line of sight, target invalidation, unload or loss of valid approach cancels it. Revalidate on the owning thread immediately before applying the lease. |
| Pause and recover | Propose an 80-tick maximum pause, non-stacking, with at most one lease per actor and target. Remain exposed briefly before retreat. A cooldown of at least 120 ticks begins after the windup resolves. No refresh while a lease is active. |
| Disengage | Stop targeting on invalid world, chunk, actor or player. Clear lease state across world unload and Save/Quit. The transfer owner must resume normally when a lease is absent, including on reopen and on restart after an abrupt exit. |

### Adapter boundary and installed pack audit

- **IC2 1.97.** The pinned installation's `EnergyNet` exposes TileEntity add/remove operations. `TileEntityCable` couples registration to its lifecycle and the native `TileEntityCableSplitter` changes registration with its own redstone state. Those operations have cache and topology implications. A normal cable has no verified external pause lease. First test a ZTO-owned source withholding new EU offers or a documented native control path with explicit owner opt-in. Require native re-registration, alternate route and conservation evidence before proposing support for any cable.
- **BuildCraft 2.2.14.** The installed `TileGenericPipe` delegates updates and serialization to its pipe. `PipeTransportItems` tracks and serializes traveling payloads, and `PipeTransportLiquids` maintains side and center buffers. The transport includes a pipe-owned liquid hook, which applies to an implementation that owns that pipe. The existing `ILiquidContainer` endpoint contract cannot pause an arbitrary installed pipe. Test a verified sender gating new offers with queued items, partial fills, backpressure and an alternate route. Treat item pipes and fluid pipes as separate adapters.
- **RedPower 2.0pr5b2.** Installed wiring has connection and cached state, while BluePower tracks conductor state. Neither is an accepted intervention surface. A player-installed control input is a possible future target only if its ordinary state and automation semantics can be preserved. Start with observational coverage.

The adapter must bind one explicit loaded target identity and return bounded rejection reasons such as `UNSUPPORTED_TARGET`, `CHUNK_UNLOADED`, `NO_APPROACH`, `TARGET_CHANGED` and `ALREADY_PAUSED`. Admission must leave the block, TileEntity identity, metadata, inventory and network membership intact. Revalidate identity on apply and expire, and use an automatic finite deadline on the owning thread. Lease state must be transient. Reopening the same world or replacing the target must resume normal transfer without an attempt to restore a stale snapshot. Crash, unload and Peaceful cleanup must also be safe, since a replacement or chunk transition can happen before ordinary expiry. Unsupported adapters produce visual diversion only.

Before enabling an adapter, reconcile inventories including in-flight item payloads, total fluid by type, EU offered and accepted, routing and deliberate normal losses before, during and after the pause. Test overlap, repeated interruption, bidirectional networks, power cycling, block removal and replacement, chunk spillover, dimension changes, Save/Quit, abrupt restart and absence of the optional mod. Reject the adapter if any path can lose, duplicate or silently redirect resources, or force a chunk load.

## Tank prototype

Use a broad, heavy silhouette and a slow, visible approach. This role occupies open lanes and gives other hostiles a moving point of pressure. At an accessible chokepoint it pauses, commits to a forward strike and then briefly exposes its flank during recovery. The Breacher spends time striking a barrier in close quarters. The Tank needs room to turn and commit, gives ground to flanking, and presents a priority target in open space. Concentrated ranged fire, traps, stronger Tekkit equipment and a retreat through a narrow passage should all remain effective.

- Start with one opt-in prototype, a clearance envelope roughly 1.4 blocks wide and 2.6 tall, and one active Tank at most. These dimensions are test inputs. Match model, collision and navigation, verify doorway and low-ceiling rejection, and reject insertion if the loaded lane has no route to a useful engagement position.
- Use a fixed, limited path refresh cadence and a threshold for giving up on repeated stalls. Telegraph one frontal attack for roughly 30 ticks, lock direction on commitment, use short reach checked at impact and follow with a recovery interval. No attacks through solid cover. Avoid body-blocking all exits, block destruction, broad damage immunity and attack tracking that defeats dodging.
- Spend an existing active slot and an existing wave allocation. Propose a 30-second engagement before releasing encounter targeting, with bounded retry and no chunk loading. Test rendering, collision, target release, narrow fences and trenches, melee, arrows, traps, an advanced IC2/EE2 loadout and loss of the player across a portal. Save/reopen must preserve the entity's stable identity and ordinary health while clearing stale attack, player ownership and path state. Verify normal Peaceful removal and disengagement from Creative players.
- Begin comparisons using the Breacher's health for both roles, with the same ordinary support and terrain. Choose final speed, resilience, damage and loot only after movement and counterplay trials. The prototype should change player positioning through attack and collision. Test any subsequent increase in health for the time it buys the rest of the assault.

## Encounter composition and sequencing

Propose a shared special-role budget of at most two substitutions per player assault and one per wave, with one slot at low encounter budgets. A later director can choose from a weighted role pool after checking terrain, player-facing counterplay, territorial risk, recent role history and population. Each confirmed role spends an ordinary allocation. Give a Skirmisher room to reposition, pair one frontline threat with ranged pressure in open space, and avoid selecting both Tank and Breacher for the same narrow entrance. Consider only one disruption threat at a time. Keep fallback to ordinary slots when a selected role runs out of placement options. A seeded slate can support diversity and cooldowns without rolling every role independently.

- **0.2:** calibrate Skirmisher and Breacher. Prototype the Tank's placement and exposure and the Saboteur's navigation and interruption in separate disposable tests. A player-vicinity assault can use a terrain-compatible Tank or a conspicuous distraction without choosing a player asset.
- **0.3:** establish what counts as a persistent site and how protected interiors, exposed equipment and old worlds are recognized. Investigate IC2 and BuildCraft independently with conservation fixtures. Avoid selecting industrial targets by proximity to the player alone.
- **0.4:** consider a `SITE_SIEGE` Saboteur with an individually verified adapter and recognized exposed site target. Consider a Tank-led wave with known approaches and retreat space. Site target ownership and authorization belong to the site encounter. The lunar calendar remains an event source.
