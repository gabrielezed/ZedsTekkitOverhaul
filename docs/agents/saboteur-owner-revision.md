# Alpha.5 Saboteur owner-feedback revision

The owner's Survival playtest identified three problems in the original PR #34 prototype. The Saboteur read as friendly, was easy to pin down, and could gesture at machinery without affecting the owner's BuildCraft or IC2 lines. The revised prototype keeps `ZTO.Saboteur`, spawn egg stack ID `22262` and its native health and NBT. Its 0.45 × 1.1 block hull, 12 HP and one-point melee attack keep its role small and light. Natural lunar composition, registrations for other roles and site ownership remain at their existing boundaries.

The new geometry has a narrow torso, forward-set six-texel head, squared jaw, longer arms and separate talon-like fingers. An asymmetric gait and idle hand motion add restlessness. A two-handed reach and violet link denote machine windup. A one-handed gesture and particles centered on the entity identify the decoy. A dropped shoulder and swept-back hands denote a successful escape. A hit can interrupt the windup before a short window for evasion.

## Mobility and counterplay

Approach and evasion choose different destinations. Approach tests up to three forward or diagonal lanes. Evasion can follow a landed hit of at least two damage (two chances in three), eight continuous ticks of visible melee pressure, or an interrupted windup. It tries backward, diagonal and lateral lanes, then a shorter retreat, with at most six candidates. Its landing must increase separation and be at least three horizontal blocks from the threat. Both modes share the successful blink timer, with 32 ticks after approach and 38 after evasion. A failed search waits eight ticks before either mode searches again. Hits apply normally before an eligible evasion and a lethal hit ends the encounter.

The validator checks loaded chunks with a halo, legal feet height, a continuously swept collision hull, normal-cube support under that hull, hazards and an unoccupied landing. A broad-phase query returns native collision shapes. The sweep checks only where the body actually crosses those shapes, which allows a diagonal past an open corner. Closed doors, walls and gaps still block travel. The blink stays on one level and native pathing handles elevation changes. The Saboteur cannot use a teleport to cross a sealed barrier or a world boundary.

## BuildCraft 2.2.14 and IC2 boundary

The Tekkit Dev `buildcraft-client-B-transport-2.2.14.zip` provides the tested behavior. `TileGenericPipe` binds the pipe, runs scheduled neighbor changes and the power provider, delegates updates, and serializes the pipe ID and native pipe data. Invalidation removes the pipe from the persistent tile registry. `PipeTransportItems` owns and serializes traveling payloads, changes their ownership across handoffs and can drop them when there is no receiving path or when the pipe is removed. `PipeTransportLiquids` owns independent side and center buffers, their quantities, IDs and travel queues, and serializes them together. Both hook types are decided by the pipe's own implementation. A generic pause of native transport would require intervention inside BuildCraft. Changing connections or replacing pipes also triggers native topology and removal behavior.

The supported setup is a **ZTO Distillation Receiver feeding one BuildCraft wooden liquid pipe set to extract from that receiver**. The receiver must have stock and have drained recently. Its side binding checks the loaded pipe tile, exact wood-liquid pipe implementation and intake direction. `PipeLiquidsWood.updateEntity` probes `ILiquidContainer.empty(..., false)`, puts the accepted amount into BuildCraft's buffer and then commits exactly that amount. ZTO gates the receiver's existing `empty` query and commit for up to 80 simulation ticks. The pipe and its power, topology, fluid buffers, accepted amounts and native serialization stay under BuildCraft ownership. Buffered fluid can finish traveling. The receiver panel shows the remaining pause in ticks. The receiver emits the world-space disruption effect only after a nonempty extraction offer is blocked. Intake, production and direct player bucket transfers through the boiler keep their ordinary behavior. The legacy `empty` API has no caller or side argument, so the pause applies to automation extracting through this receiver's API. Multiple adjacent wooden extractors make the receiver ineligible.

Normal wooden liquid pipes with a stock BuildCraft tank, generic item and liquid pipes, and unverified endpoints remain unsupported. An item adapter would need a sender-side hook that declines a native extraction before the source inventory mutates and preserves ordinary insertion. Its fixture would reconcile the source slots, traveling payloads and receiver slots before and after the pause. IC2 1.97 `TileEntityCable` registers with EnergyNet through its own lifecycle, and the native splitter changes registration under redstone control. An IC2 adapter needs a source-owned gate on new EU offers or a separately verified native control path. There is no EnergyNet re-registration, cable metadata change or IC2 interruption in this revision.

The lease is exclusive and transient. It is checked against the loaded receiver and extraction configuration and loses authority when the actor dies, stops ticking in the world or enters Peaceful cleanup. Both receiver unload and invalidation discard the lease. Nothing is written into the receiver NBT for an active disruption. The Hopper's existing transfer gate and NBT remain compatible. Future site encounters can select these narrow transfer boundaries without changing lunar or territorial authority.

The fixtures copy Tekkit Dev into a separate profile with a marked disposable save. They verify muted audio on a private desktop. `saboteur.nearby` returns the target type, pose, last cause and result, successful cooldown, failed-search backoff and cumulative counts, bounded to four live records. `machine.inspect` on the receiver reports reserve, liquid ID, remaining lease ticks and blocked offers. Success in the BuildCraft fixture requires native tank delivery to stop after in-flight fluid has drained, then resume.

The open PR baseline was `f5105856f8a28384eb8e1e1ee81e598c953c21e6` (JAR SHA-256 `a102764e60faa52dd7ce32ffb4fc29e502a50f3d4152efcf99d1a730255401d3`). The runtime reported Minecraft 1.2.5, FML 2.2.106.176, Forge 3.3.8.164 and Java 1.8.0_51.

In five controlled Survival engagements, the baseline blinked once and the revision blinked six times, or 4.7 versus 25.9 per simulated minute and 0.2 versus 1.2 per engagement. The revision escaped laterally beside a wall and rejected every escape in the sealed corridor. A player repositioned into reach to anticipate the escape could still finish it. In the open encounter where the player held position, the revision survived the 100-tick observation with 7 HP. The baseline died at tick 77. Three real projectiles landed on each version. In a separate trial on unchanged generated grass, the revision traveled 4.86 blocks and climbed from Y=65 to Y=66 using native navigation between blinks. These samples compare the same short fixtures. The owner playtest remains the balance check for unrestricted combat.

The final BuildCraft line conserved 6,000 mB of gasoline, liquid ID 3017, on each of the supported and standalone control lines. The activated lease began with 420 mB in flight. During a 79-tick observed pause, the receiver declined 38 extraction probes. Its source stayed at 5,000 mB. The receiving tank settled at 1,000 mB as the pipe finished draining, then held for 57 ticks. The standalone control line continued, and the supported line delivered fresh fluid within 33 ticks of expiry. Authenticated Bridge reads observed the receiver target, windup, blocked offers and expiry. Native Save and Quit and a separate process reopening after an abrupt halt returned the receiver to normal delivery with all fluid accounted for. The lifecycle checkpoints were taken during an active lease after the existing buffer had drained. Tee routing and arbitrary installed pipes remain outside this fixture.

The Hopper exercise passed with and without Bridge. The eight-actor unsupported-target fixture still observed harmless diversions.
