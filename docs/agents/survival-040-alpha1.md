# Survival 0.4.0-alpha.1 — Site-directed perimeter approach

Status: opt-in singleplayer candidate for Tekkit Classic 3.1.2. The [authoritative specification](survival-040-spec.md) defines the approved first-alpha boundary and preserves later proposals. The recognized home becomes a stable objective. Walls, openings and terrain shape the encounter, and a sealed perimeter can defeat it without structural loss.

## Admission and target

`LocalLunarCalendar` ticks the existing settlement service before the encounter consumer. `SiegeDirector` owns the single major admission decision at the natural Overworld full-moon dusk. A pending plan reserves that opportunity in memory. Successful preflight commits the shared major boundary before native insertion. Failed preflight falls back to the established player assault. Resynchronization cancels the pending decision. An interrupted committed siege keeps its boundary.

Use `experimentalSiteSieges=true` with `playerAssault=true` to opt in. Otherwise the branch preserves the default player assault path. The provisional opportunity selects one of three logical lunar cycles by a stable residue derived from the world seed. It uses the existing eight-day calendar. A suitable route may wait at most 900 ticks for native darkness, with bounded retries. The opportunity is still the original natural dusk. A natural dusk with no suitable site falls through immediately.

`SiteSiege` discovers recognized sites through `SiteQueries.nearby` within 24 blocks. It ranks usable parts by distance to their bounds with the save-local site ID as tie-break. It evaluates at most two parts. A dormant, stale or unavailable site cannot admit. A PARTIAL site can supply a usable part when the approach itself verifies in live loaded terrain. The admission observation carries site, dimension and session. The controller resolves those identities and the selected part again before insertion and during the encounter. Player position supplies initial discovery, Survival eligibility and a 48-block simulation range from the selected part.

## Geometry and movement

`SiteApproach` evaluates up to twelve straight cardinal lanes per part. The start is fourteen blocks from the part face and outside the horizontal envelope plus a two-block exclusion margin. A second nearby query checks other recognized envelopes at insertion. A route can reach an open doorway or end at verified exterior frontage. It uses the actual loaded heightmap, standing-height clearance, solid ground and open sky at the insertion column. The native door state and collision shape affect route clearance. Cold, covered, unloaded or uncertain plans defer or fail closed. Protected Interior must report EXPOSED before every insertion. EXPOSED alone gives no permission to spawn.

The final placement reuses native ground, crowding, collision, liquid and ordinary hostile spawn checks. It allows at most three stock skeletons, one group and seven insertion attempts at twenty-tick spacing. The local hostile cap is 28. The site does not claim aerial slots or tactical Enderkin. Existing player assaults keep their configured placement and composition.

`PerimeterApproachTask` attaches to stock skeletons through the verified MCP and pinned client action-task field. A temporary high-priority task uses native movement toward sequential world-space waypoints. The task never sets position after initial insertion. A route can enter an open threshold after starting outside. Solid collisions, support and observed obstruction bound progress. The controller releases the approach task on arrival, allowing normal skeleton combat and despawn. Encounter termination releases all remaining tasks and discards tracking. Ordinary loaded or saved skeletons retain their native lifecycle.

The straight, level lane model supports suitable surface entrances and frontage. Stairs, irregular turns, unsupported legacy block shapes, and vertical or underground approaches commonly defer or fall back. Closed walls can yield a frontage objective on their exterior. A blocked lane never authorizes an interior insertion.

## Persistence and budgets

The existing `data/zto_player_assault.dat` uses its original `Boundary` and `Open` tags. A versioned optional `SiteSiege` compound stores the last site ID, original admission boundary, last outcome, and up to 128 site cooldowns. Each commitment reserves at least 384,000 raw ticks for that site. The consumed boundary rejects replays after rewind or reload. Expired cooldowns are pruned on new commitments, and a full live table defers further site admissions. Legacy data without the optional compound loads normally. A malformed siege extension disables siege admission while keeping the valid major watermark and player assault path.

On reopening an open siege, the candidate records `INTERRUPTED_ON_REOPEN` and retains the cooldown and consumed boundary. It persists no route, snapshot or mob pointer. During play it resolves on attacker loss, exhausted insertion, stopped progress, unexpected route obstruction, six hundred unpaused ticks, player absence, Peaceful, site invalidation, world changes or lunar cancellation. Once all inserted attackers reach frontage, normal local combat can continue and the encounter resolves after at least 240 ticks.

Planning runs on the owning world thread. It samples one lane at a time, up to 24 lanes and 40 planning samples excluding bounded twilight deferrals. One sample can examine at most 28 standing columns, one heightmap lookup and 255 sky cells. Protection uses its existing idle recognition slot and existing 169-read ceiling. Route revalidation samples only upcoming waypoints. The planner performs no general pathfinder search or synchronous chunk generation. World access first checks chunk availability. Native entity updates continue under the vanilla lifecycle.

## Diagnostics and verification

Open **Numpad 1 → Site Siege**. The monitor reports current major encounter kind, opportunity and boundary, planning phase and failure, recognized site ID and coverage, site session and dimension, insertion and destination, objective progress, active and inserted attackers, remaining budget, elapsed ticks, outcome and cooldown. The immutable snapshot also exposes peak attackers, movement progress, geometry age, lane and sample counts, protection reads and planner timings. Normal gameplay receives no overlay.

Run `zto-tools/test-zto-agent.ps1 -Focus site-siege` after building. The runner uses marked disposable worlds and the existing natural lunar listener, pinned native entity logic and actual save storage. For deterministic admission it computes an eligible cycle in the fixture and begins at the tick before dusk. The production game exposes no force-siege command. Run `zto-tools/test-zto-agent.ps1 -Pack` for the full headless compatibility and Survival suite. See [test tiers and graphical host restriction](testing.md).

## Owner walkthrough

1. Use a disposable Tekkit Dev copy. Set `experimentalSiteSieges=true` and leave `playerAssault=true` in `minecraft/config/ZedsTekkitOverhaul.cfg`. Restart in Survival in the Overworld on Normal or Hard. Keep the approach dark and find level terrain outside the house. Use **Settlement Recognition → Assessment** after walking inside and using the house to confirm a positive ID. Wait for natural dusk on a selected full-moon cycle. The dusk tick is 13000. The selected cycle is `floorMod(cycleIndex, 3) == floorMod(seed ^ (seed >>> 32), 3)`, where `cycleIndex` comes from the existing lunar calendar. Keep the clock advancing normally across dusk. Use independent disposable copies for repeat cases so cooldowns and prior boundaries are reproducible.
2. For an ordinary house, stay near its center during admission. In **Site Siege**, watch for PLANNING, then ACTIVE and SITE_SIEGE. The start of the reported approach must be outside the settled bounds. Observe ordinary skeletons walking toward the recorded frontage. Confirm steps and progress before resolution. Record the site ID, coverage and final outcome.
3. Build a perimeter with one walkable opening and a straight, level exterior lane. Keep at least twelve blocks between the observer and the insertion end. Verify attackers walk along the approach from outside, with OPENING when a supported threshold was selected. Move laterally away from the entrance while remaining within 48 blocks of the part. Confirm that both the site ID and path remain fixed while nearby combat remains possible after approach.
4. Repeat with the entrance sealed before dusk. Expect an exterior frontage or a rejected approach. The walls and doorway should remain intact. Close a doorway during an active approach in a separate run and observe the bounded route failure when it enters the next sampled waypoints.
5. Reopen a recognized compound or use the status in Settlement Recognition to find one with PARTIAL coverage. Stay near a loaded, observed part while its other bounds remain incomplete. Confirm that a verified exterior lane can admit, or that the monitor gives the relevant geometric rejection. Check that an exposed internal courtyard is never the insertion point.
6. Leave the area during approach. Confirm `PLAYER_ABSENT`, no active attackers under siege authority, and a cooldown. In a separate run, save and quit during ACTIVE. Reopen and confirm there is no new insertion, the cooldown remains, and `INTERRUPTED_ON_REOPEN` is visible if the last save captured the open encounter.

## Next integration points

The director can arbitrate later tactical profiles within the same boundary. A later planner can propose alternate objectives through the same immutable site observations and action-specific geometry checks. Breaching, industrial infiltration, aerial participation, new enemies, advanced groups and multiplayer require separate approval and validation. Owner acceptance should cover gameplay pacing, actual wall and entrance behavior, normal play without diagnostic overlays, and monitor readability across GUI scales.
