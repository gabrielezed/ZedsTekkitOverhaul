# Phase 2 · Baseline and industrial profile proposal

The captured Tekkit Dev catalog is `dist-zto/ZedsTekkitOverhaul-emc-baseline.tsv`, generated from the installed client with EE2 external integration and ZTO compatibility defaults active. It contains 149,618 rows, including 34,942 positive rows (358 direct and 34,584 durability-derived). The SHA-256 is `5AE1FA07391BA96389B66C7345183A83891EFCCD17985BF60EB12AF1F1FA7E21`. [Audit procedure and artifact versions](emc-audit.md).

The catalog joins registered items, creative variants, direct EE2 keys and the durability range. Archive and class identify the implementation and can differ from the original owning mod when a class is replaced. The map retains the final direct value and carries no per-write provenance. The catalog has gaps for hidden metadata outside the enumerated sources, NBT-dependent identities and behavior, and item variants listed exclusively by external recipe or NEI plugins. Enumeration and name failures are marked per row. Positive damage rows represent alternate states of a single item. They do not represent separate material definitions. The catalog includes zero-valued rows for ores, tool and machine progression, BC Fuel and ZTO fractions.

## Before and proposed after

[Industrial profile for review](emc-industrial-proposal.txt) inherits every unlisted mapping. Its single economic change is removing the one-EMC renewable water filling premium while preserving the empty bucket value. Values are per item at metadata 0 unless otherwise stated.

## Balances and regression cases

Use integer item counts and exact input and output quantities. A closed loop must restore every material, catalyst and template to its initial count and include every energy source and sink. Compare EMC, EU, BuildCraft energy and mB as separate coordinates. A loop powered by sunlight or renewable water has a positive external resource flow even if its monetary input is zero. Decide separately which renewable production paths are desirable.

| Fixture | Material and energy ledger | Expected observation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cobble, furnace, tablet or condenser | 64 cobble (64 EMC) plus 12,800 uninterrupted furnace ticks becomes 64 stone (64 EMC). Eight coal at 1,600 ticks each supply a full batch. Account for consumed fuel and any wasted burn. An electric furnace can substitute EU, and Silk Touch can acquire stone directly. | No material EMC premium for furnace smelting, electric smelting or obtaining stone directly. Test stone → cobble by ordinary breaking and repeat the exchange loop with a template held aside. |
| Crafting, 5×5, Foundry | 4 workbenches (128 EMC) → 1 table (128 EMC). 16 smooth stone (16 EMC) → 1 Foundry (16 EMC), table retained. Four logs yield the four workbenches. Multiply by whole recipe batches before comparing outputs. | The condenser template stays present and must be excluded from net outputs. Repeated crafting and exchange should break even before any energy or fuel expense. |
| IC2 ore doubling and smelting | 1 iron ore → 2 dust → 2 ingots (512 EMC); 1 iron dust → 1 ingot (256 EMC). Tin follows the same value balance, copper yields 170 EMC for two units. Maceration and optional electric smelting consume measured EU from the machine tier and upgrades used. | Mining supplies new ore and explains the return. If any variant becomes exchangeable, validate every substitute ore against the cheapest path and twice the dust output, including Nether ore preprocessing and recycling. Test reversible ingot and dust paths with solar EU. |
| Vanilla buckets | Bucket (768) + 1,000 mB water → water bucket (769). Emptying returns the same bucket and releases 1,000 mB. The analogous lava premium is 64, with 20,000 furnace ticks per lava bucket and legacy container recovery. | Renewable water filling already gives +1 EMC whenever the filled bucket is exchanged. Confirm lava recovery per device, and compare geothermal conversion separately. Never add the recovered bucket again to a filled-bucket EMC value. |
| Oil, generic refinery and fractions | One full bucket supplies 1,000 mB crude and returns its empty container. Every ZTO batch consumes 250 mB crude and yields 250 mB of one selected fraction, with no byproduct. Gasoline / kerosene / diesel / heavy gas oil consume respectively 5,120 / 6,400 / 8,320 / 10,240 EU and 160 / 200 / 240 / 320 mB cooling water per 1,000 mB output. A second bucket used to withdraw is a reusable container. The BC refinery converts 1,000 mB crude into 1,000 mB generic Fuel for 10,000 BuildCraft energy before external losses. Generic Fuel has 250,000 nominal combustion energy per bucket. | Validate four ZTO batches per bucket, pipe and tank conservation, and placement and re-collection at source metadata. EE2 oil duplication can still bypass crude extraction. The refinery and engine can consume part of their product to sustain refining while drawing down crude. The four ZTO fractions have no engine entry. |
| EE2 fuel and IC2 electricity | Furnace tick inputs of 25,600 / 102,400 / 409,600 yield theoretical uninterrupted smelts of 128 / 512 / 2,048. The Generator divides by 4 and, at 10 EU per active tick, produces 64,000 / 256,000 / 1,024,000 EU before external losses and assuming room to emit. | Consuming exchangeable fuel to produce EU is an intentional one-way edge. Test any proposed EU-powered route back to exchangeable materials with the spent EMC, catalyst and container balance included. The observed ratios are recipe properties. |
| Collector and alternate fuel recipes | 4 × 512 = 2,048 and 4 × 2,048 = 8,192. Check reverse recipe quantities and catalyst return. The collector changes one item to a successor and charges the difference in live values, using its generated EMC. | An inverted or zero-price collector edge can bypass the intended ladder. Validate collector variants before enabling any fuel price override. |

Use floor division for per-output allocation when an integer EMC price must fit an input budget, and check the residual at actual craft size. Carry residual mB within the tank and round only at the bucket interface. Treat a nonexchangeable input as a progression gate. It cannot be substituted as zero cost in a material-only cycle solver.

Decisions before default adoption include whether to close the baseline water premium, whether to gate oil extraction by changing its EE2 fuel classification, whether condensed EE2 fuels should power industry at their present rates, and whether other routes such as Nether ores and installed energy converters reopen these edges. The table is a targeted proposal with inheritance for the rest of the catalog. A complete installed recipe graph, observed machine throughput, and closure over cross-mod alternatives remain open. Disabling BuildCraft Fuel would require a separate recipe, engine and saved-liquid policy.
